Today vs. the proposal

Sincerely: today's Animtool is powerful and fast for someone who knows it. It is not easy for someone who doesn't - and a studio has many of those.

What gets in the way
Hard to find. 57 tools in 6 tabs; the Keys tab alone has 11 sections. An animator learns where things are by memory, not by sight.
Icons without words. The 24 toolbar icons are small and unlabelled; several look alike (Mirror / Flip, the tangents).
No feedback. Nothing says what is selected, which frame or key you're between, or what a button will do before you press it.
Hidden power. Many options live in right-click menus (scope, amounts, 'both hands') - great once known, invisible to a new animator.
It crops. Docked narrow next to the Channel Box (where animators keep it), rows are cut off.
GUS ANIMTOOL · 5.8 PROPOSAL

The principles - and what we borrow from other departments

No new tools. Better ways to show and reach the 57 we have, taken from places where they already work.

Six principles
1

One place per job

Five areas in the order you animate: Pick & see → Pose → Keys & timing → Spaces & props → Limbs. Each has its colour.

2

Always know the context

Character, frame range, what's selected and where you are between keys - always on top.

3

See it before you press it

Every button says what it will do ('Key the pose at frame 14'); pictures show what a tool changes.

4

The power stays one click away

Right-click options become visible choices on the card (scope, amounts) - the right-click still works.

5

Your bar, your way

Profiles (Blocking, Polish...), drag, add, remove, row / column / grid.

6

Nothing lost, nothing cut

All 57 tools, the same actions and hotkeys; a test checks every one and every width.

Borrowed (presentation only - nothing new to learn twice)
Editorial (NLE timelines)Timing shown as a timeline

Inbetweens, offsets and retimes show the keys moving on a little timeline - the way editors see a cut ripple.

Compositing (node colours)Colour = family

Like nodes coloured by type, every tool keeps its area's colour - on the window, the bar, the menus.

Game engines (context panels)Show what fits the selection

Like a details panel that changes with what you pick: arm controls selected → the arm's tools first (IK/FK, mirror).

Music software (transport bar)The shot strip

A strip that never moves: character, frames, play range, Picker / Poses / Clips - like a DAW's transport.

Layout / previsShot information

The shot's name and range from the scene file on the strip, so a playblast or a bake can't use the wrong range.

Pose libraries with thumbnailsPictures, not names

Poses and clips as thumbnails (the pose library already saves pictures) - bigger, searchable, in the Pose area.

3D apps' radial menusA pie on a hotkey (variant)

Optional: hold a key, the bar's profile opens around the cursor - the same tools, no reach to the bar.

Accessibility (UI standards)Colour is never the only cue

Each area also has a number and a shape; a high-contrast variant for long days and colour-blind animators.

Honest: what could go wrong, and how we handle it

A pitch that hides the flaws doesn't survive the first week in production.

Risks
Muscle memory. Animators who know today's tabs will look in the old places. → the search finds any tool; the old window stays one click away until every area is done; hotkeys don't change.
Space. Pictures and cards take room; animators dock narrow. → Compact variant, pictures fold into '?', the sidebar shrinks to icons; the bar is where daily work happens.
The next-step lamp can nag. Useful for new people, noise for experts. → it only suggests, never acts; a switch in Settings turns it off.
Colour-blind users. Areas told apart by colour alone would fail them. → numbers and shapes on every area; the High-contrast variant.
Speed in heavy shots. A Qt window that follows the selection costs a little on every click. → only the visible page updates; measured in Test this release (the Rigtool window opens in 0.25 s on your Mac).
Pie menu / HUD are new ways in. They are not new tools, but they are new things to learn and to maintain. → variants, off by default; build them only if you want them.
Maintenance. Pictures per tool are more to keep up to date. → the pages are drawn from the tools' own code (as in the Rigtool); a test checks every tool is there.
Honest unknown. Maya 2025 (PySide6) behaves a bit differently from 2024. → both are supported; your Test this release runs on 2024 - a 2025 run would be wise before a studio rollout.
How we'd know it works
Clicks to a tool: today 2-4 (tab, section, sometimes a right-click) → 1 with the bar or search, 2 in the window.
Time to find a tool for a new animator: test it: give 10 tasks to someone new, time it on the old and the new window.
Nothing lost: 57 of 57 tools, every option, every hotkey - checked by a test, like the Rigtool's 332 controls.
Rollout (each step: your preview OK → build → your tests; the old window until the end)
